# SphereBrain Research Philosophy

## Core idea

SphereBrain explores the hypothesis that intelligence may not need to be represented internally as language, symbolic facts, or explicit semantic labels.

External information can arrive as language, images, sound, or other modalities. An Encoder may convert that information into numerical stimulation. The SphereBrain Core then changes through the activity generated by repeated experience.

The working hypothesis is:

> **Intelligence is an evolving structure of recognition shaped by experience.**

## Meaning is not assumed inside the Core

Humans may describe two experiences as having the same meaning. The Core does not necessarily possess that human description.

For the Core, an experience is a pattern of stimulation followed by internal activity. Over time, different experiences may:

- reuse some of the same paths,
- diverge into different paths,
- converge on shared internal regions,
- strengthen recurrent structures,
- create distributed support,
- preserve or lose structures over time,
- change how later experiences propagate.

A sufficiently persistent and reusable internal structure may correspond to what humans later call a concept or meaning, but that interpretation must come **after** the structure is observed.

## Language is a source of experience, not the Core's native thought

Language contains an enormous combinatorial space of relationships. SphereBrain does not require the Core to manipulate words as its fundamental units.

A working direction is:

```text
Language / image / sound / environment
                ↓
             Encoder
                ↓
        numerical stimulation
                ↓
          SphereBrain Core
                ↓
      evolving internal structure
                ↓
             Decoder
```

The Encoder and Decoder may themselves use language-aware AI systems. This does not imply that the Core's internal organization is linguistic.

## Structure before interpretation

The project increasingly distinguishes two viewpoints.

### Human-side viewpoint

Humans can label inputs as:

- animal,
- vehicle,
- sky,
- movement,
- tool,
- similar,
- different.

These labels are useful for designing controlled experiments.

### Core-side viewpoint

The Core only exposes measurable internal consequences such as:

- activated nodes,
- traversed edges,
- activation distributions,
- path reuse,
- convergence,
- separation,
- persistence,
- recurrence,
- distributed support.

The research goal is not to force the Core to recreate the human labels. It is to test whether internal organization emerges from experience and whether that organization later maps onto useful regularities in the world.

## What would count as meaningful evidence?

Strong evidence would not be merely that one input activates one expected node.

More interesting evidence would include:

1. different experiences independently converging on a reusable internal structure;
2. that structure persisting across time and additional experience;
3. new experiences reusing the structure without being explicitly directed to do so;
4. damage to the structure affecting multiple related behaviors rather than a single memorized case;
5. structural organization emerging before human semantic labels are consulted;
6. the organization remaining observable across seeds, domains, and larger experience streams.

## What SphereBrain does not currently claim

SphereBrain does **not** currently claim to have demonstrated:

- human-like understanding,
- consciousness,
- general intelligence,
- a complete model of biological brains,
- a replacement for large language models,
- a proven universal theory of meaning.

The project is an experimental investigation of a narrower hypothesis: whether experience-driven internal structure can become a functional substrate for recognition, memory, and eventually higher-order organization.

## Research attitude

The project intentionally preserves failed experiments and contradictory results.

A hypothesis that fails is not removed from the record. Instead, the failure should narrow the next question.

The aim is not to make SphereBrain appear correct. The aim is to discover what the Core actually does.
