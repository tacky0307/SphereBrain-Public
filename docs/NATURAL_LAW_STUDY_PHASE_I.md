# SphereBrain Natural Law Study — Phase I

## Experience-shaped dynamics in an artificial world

This report freezes the first major Natural Law Study milestone of SphereBrain.

The working question is not whether the Core can already understand language or reproduce human cognition. The narrower question is:

> If experience changes the physical-like internal state of an artificial network, what kinds of history-dependent dynamics emerge before semantic interpretation is imposed?

Phase I follows the research path from early natural-law tests through the internal-dynamics studies up to ID1R.

## Research stance

SphereBrain treats the Core as an artificial dynamical world rather than as a symbolic knowledge store.

The current working picture is:

```text
Experience
   ↓
Activity / Motion
   ↓
Flow
   ↓
Landscape deformation
   ↓
Consolidation / recovery timescale
   ↓
Future Activity
```

The system is intentionally not given explicit semantic labels, memory lookup tables, reward targets, context IDs, sequence IDs, or timestamps for the studies summarized here.

## What was introduced deliberately

The Natural Law studies use a minimal physical-like model containing:

- Nodes and Edges as the structural substrate
- Activity / Motion as the transient state
- local Flow generated by state differences and conductance
- Landscape deformation driven by Flow
- finite shared structural resource
- natural recovery / decay
- later, local Consolidation driven only by local Flow, which slows local recovery

Consolidation is a persistent local material variable. It is not a memory label, repeat counter, semantic category, or reward signal.

## What was not introduced

The studies in this report do not add:

- semantic meaning inside the Core
- explicit memory records or retrieval operations
- correct-answer targets or reward learning
- importance labels
- repetition counters used by the law
- context identifiers
- sequence identifiers
- timestamps interpreted by the law

The distinction between imposed rules and observed phenomena is central to interpretation.

## Main observations

### 1. Flow can deform the future dynamics of the world

Experience-driven Flow can alter the Landscape, and the altered Landscape changes later dynamics even after transient activity is reset.

This supports causal history dependence through persistent structure. It does not by itself establish memory or cognition.

### 2. Simultaneous stimuli can produce non-additive whole states

When two stimuli are present together, the resulting plastic Whole-State and rested Landscape can differ from the sum of the two single-stimulus histories.

In the tested pair set, all 15 simultaneous pairs showed interaction-level non-additivity under plastic dynamics, while the non-plastic control remained additive to numerical precision.

This suggests that a relation-like effect can arise from interaction within the world rather than from an explicitly stored relation label.

### 3. Repetition can produce maturation and saturation

Repeated pair experience caused the Landscape and the corresponding Whole-State response to approach a stable or slowly changing region under the tested conditions.

The first repeated-pair maturation audit classified all 15 tested pairs as saturating or converging within the tested horizon.

This is described as maturation rather than as established memory.

### 4. A single-timescale recovery law erased mature and novel histories

Long natural rest under the earlier recovery law erased not only one-shot novelty but also previously matured Landscape structure.

This negative result was important: strong deformation alone was not sufficient for long-lived history.

### 5. Metaplasticity created different memory-lifetime regimes

A minimal local Consolidation variable was then introduced:

```text
local Flow
   ↓
local Consolidation
   ↓
slower local natural recovery
```

A 30-point gain × consolidation-decay sweep produced observational regions labeled:

- forgetting: 17 / 30
- transient memory: 8 / 30
- long-lived structure: 5 / 30

These are descriptive bins, not claims of a mathematical phase transition.

### 6. Repetition changed lifetime without a repetition counter

When repetition count was swept more finely, long-rest retention generally rose smoothly and then saturated rather than jumping at a single discrete repetition threshold.

Across 10 boundary-focused worlds, no conservative sharp-lifetime-transition signal was detected.

The result suggests that experience lifetime can depend continuously on the interaction between Flow-driven consolidation, consolidation decay, and repeated exposure, without the law counting repetitions.

### 7. Timing of otherwise identical experience mattered

With identical A+B experience count fixed at 10 and only the inter-episode rest changed, 3 of 4 tested worlds showed a temporal-spacing effect.

In the tested metaplastic dynamics, overly long spacing often weakened maturation because Landscape and Consolidation decayed before the next experience arrived.

The law did not receive a clock value or schedule label. Timing mattered because the world itself changed during the interval.

### 8. Empty time and lived experience were not equivalent

For the same first A+B, same final A+B, and equalized elapsed interval, inserting another experience produced a different later A+B response than rest alone in all 3 tested worlds.

The strongest tested intervening condition was consistently A+C, followed by C+D and E+F in the reported worlds.

This is interpreted as primitive history/context dependence, not semantic context understanding.

### 9. Experience order sometimes left a sequence imprint

ID1R compared reversed orders of the same two intervening experiences while keeping experience contents, counts, spacing, total elapsed time, first A+B, and final A+B equalized.

Across 3 worlds × 3 order comparisons:

- 9 total order comparisons
- 3 supported order effects
- every tested world showed at least one order-sensitive comparison
- the repeated supported case was C+D ↔ E+F

This shows limited non-commutativity of experience operations under the tested material dynamics:

```text
F(B, F(A, S)) != F(A, F(B, S))
```

for some experience pairs.

This is called an experience-order imprint, not sequence understanding or temporal reasoning.

## Important negative results and corrections

Phase I intentionally includes failed hypotheses and corrections.

- Early free-run prediction studies did not support a predictive free-run transition under the native threshold dynamics.
- Subthreshold predictive structure was not supported in the tested v103C setup.
- Native storage was symmetric in v103D and did not support general temporal directionality.
- Matching only an accumulated Flow cosine signature did not reproduce Landscape response when input location was separated in ID1H.
- Repeated new experience in ID1L largely overwrote an earlier mature pair state under the then-current single-timescale law.
- Long rest in ID1M drove both old mature and one-shot histories toward blank under that recovery law.
- ID1O did not find a sharp repetition-count threshold for memory lifetime.
- ID1R did not show order sensitivity for every pair; only 3 of 9 reversed-order comparisons passed the conservative criterion.

These negative results shaped the later Natural Law model and are part of the evidence, not exceptions to be hidden.

## Current interpretation

The most useful working interpretation after Phase I is:

> The current SphereBrain state is not a stored list of past events. It is the material consequence of how the artificial world has been changed by its history.

Under this view:

- past Flow changes Landscape
- repeated Flow can change the recovery timescale of that Landscape
- elapsed time changes the current material state
- another experience during that interval changes the state differently than empty rest
- some sequences of experiences are non-commutative
- the same present stimulus can therefore produce different responses in differently lived worlds

This provides a concrete physical-like route toward phenomena that may later correspond to memory, context, or sequence dependence, without implementing those concepts as explicit symbolic modules.

## What Phase I does not establish

Phase I does **not** establish:

- language understanding
- semantic concepts inside the Core
- free recall
- reliable prediction
- general temporal directionality
- causal reasoning
- episodic memory
- human-like cognition
- general intelligence
- biological equivalence to the brain

The results are currently limited to the tested artificial worlds, topologies, parameters, and measurement procedures.

## Why translation is intentionally postponed

SphereBrain is not currently required to assign human meaning to A, B, or any internal state.

The research approach is to first characterize the artificial world's own regularities. If stable internal phenomena later correspond systematically to relations, context, prediction, categories, or other external-world structures, the translation layer can be designed from those observations rather than imposed in advance.

Language-aware systems such as LLMs may eventually serve as Encoder / Decoder interfaces, while the Core remains governed by its own non-linguistic dynamics.

## Phase I boundary

This report closes **Natural Law Study — Phase I** at ID1R.

The next phase should test how general these observations are rather than treating them as protected conclusions. Priority questions include:

- Do the same phenomena survive different topologies and larger worlds?
- Why do only some experience pairs show order sensitivity?
- Can multiple mature histories coexist without immediate overwrite?
- Can long-lived Landscape structure support internal dynamics without external stimulation?
- Which parts of Consolidation are genuinely necessary, and which can be reduced further?
- Can the observed material states eventually provide their own basis for translation to external meaning?

Phase I is therefore a frozen observation milestone, not a completed theory of intelligence.
